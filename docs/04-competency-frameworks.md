# Competency Frameworks for Music

What exists, across seven literatures that mostly don't cite each other — and how much of it reaches the sixteen things you want to help people learn.

v0.1 · September 2026 Curricular · psychometric · professional · informal · civic · clinical · critical 
*Before the survey*

## What a competency framework is for

Nearly every framework below exists to make something assessable — for a ministry, an accreditor, a researcher, or an exam board. That purpose is not incidental; it determines what a framework can contain. An aim that cannot be evidenced within a term, by a person who is not the learner, tends not to survive the drafting process.

This predicts the coverage pattern before we look at it. Your aims that name a capability will be well served. Your aims that name a disposition, a critique, or a change in how somebody lives will be absent — not overlooked, but structurally excluded. Worth holding onto, because it means the gaps are not an invitation to write a better framework.

*The field*  

## Seven families

*A*  

### Curricular — what schools are told to teach

Government or association documents that define music as a school subject. The most influential and the most contested.

**National Core Arts Standards**  
*United States · 2014 · NAfME / NCCAS*  

Four artistic processes, eleven anchor standards, elaborated per grade and per ensemble type. The dominant Anglophone structure, widely borrowed.

CREATING Imagine · Plan and Make · Evaluate and Refine · Present PERFORMING Select · Analyze · Interpret · Rehearse/Refine · Present RESPONDING Select · Analyze · Interpret · Evaluate CONNECTING \#10 relate personal experience to making \#11 relate works to varied contexts and daily life

**Stance** Process-based rather than repertoire-based, which was the reform. "Connecting" is the only door to anything outside music, and it is the thinnest of the four.

**Programme de formation de l'école québécoise**  
*Québec · 2001 · domaine des arts*  

Three competencies, stated as verbs, identical in shape across the four arts disciplines. Your own jurisdiction, and structurally the cleanest document here.

C1 Inventer des pièces vocales ou instrumentales C2 Interpréter des pièces musicales C3 Apprécier des œuvres musicales

**Stance** *Apprécier* explicitly carries a sociocultural dimension alongside the technical one — it is not just aesthetic judgement. That makes Québec's third competency a better hook for your *Situating* aims than anything in the American standards.

**England: National Curriculum + Model Music Curriculum**  
*United Kingdom · 2013, 2021*  

Performing, composing, listening as the statutory triad, with the 2021 Model Music Curriculum adding non-statutory repertoire lists and year-by-year expectations.

**Stance** The MMC's repertoire lists reopened exactly the argument about whose music counts that the process-based reforms were meant to settle.

*B*  

### Professional — higher education and graded ladders

Outcome statements for conservatoire degrees and the graded-exam systems that function as de facto competency ladders for millions of learners.

**AEC / Polifonia Learning Outcomes**  
*Europe · Association Européenne des Conservatoires*  

The most complete articulation of what a trained musician is supposed to be able to do, across three Bologna cycles, in three groups.

PRACTICAL artistic expression · repertoire · ensemble · practising, rehearsing, reading, aural, creative · verbal · public performance · improvisation · pedagogy THEORETICAL repertoire and materials · context · improvisation · pedagogical knowledge GENERIC independence · psychological understanding · critical awareness · communication

**Stance** Notable for treating improvisation as both a practical skill and a body of knowledge, and for putting "verbal skills — discuss music intelligently" on the same footing as playing. Third cycle asks graduates to "question conventions" and "challenge community standards", which is further than any school framework goes.

**Graded examination syllabi**  
*ABRSM · Trinity · RSL / Rockschool*  

Eight or nine graded levels per instrument, each specifying repertoire, technical work, sight-reading and aural tests. Not called competency frameworks, but functionally the most widely used ones in the world.

**Stance** RSL's popular-music grades are the interesting case — they had to invent assessment criteria for improvisation, band skills and production, which forced articulation of things classical ladders left tacit.

*C*  

### Psychometric — models built to be measured

Research instruments rather than curricula. This is where the most careful thinking about what musical ability actually consists of has happened — and where non-formal musical lives are taken most seriously.

**Goldsmiths Musical Sophistication Index**  
*Müllensiefen et al. · Goldsmiths, London*  

Five subscales measuring "the ability to engage with music in a flexible, effective and nuanced way" — deliberately broader than training, and explicitly built to be sensitive to differences *among non-musicians*.

Active musical engagement time and resources spent on music Perceptual abilities accuracy of musical listening Musical training amount of formal training Singing abilities accuracy of one's own singing Emotional engagement talking about what music expresses

**Stance** The single most MTILT-compatible instrument in this entire survey. "Active engagement" is the only place in the literature where a person who mostly listens, collects and attends is treated as having a measurable musical life. It measures rather than cultivates — but it legitimises the category.

**KoMus / KOPRA-M**  
*Germany · Jordan, Knigge, Lehmann-Wermser, Niessen et al.*  

An empirically developed and validated competence model for school music, built with the psychometric seriousness German education research applies to every subject. The core competence is named as a pair.

"Musik wahrnehmen und kontextualisieren" — perceiving AND contextualising music, as one competence KOPRA-M extends the approach to practical musical competences

**Stance** The pairing is the point, and it is unusual. Most frameworks let you perceive without contextualising. This one refuses to separate hearing a piece from knowing where it comes from — which is close to a formal statement of your own position.

**Aptitude and perception batteries**  
*Gordon · PROMS · MET*  

Gordon's audiation-based aptitude tests, the Profile of Music Perception Skills, and various discrimination batteries. Narrower, older, and mostly concerned with capacity rather than learning.

**Stance** Treats musical ability as something a person has an amount of — the frame your project is most opposed to.

*D*  

### Theoretical — models of musical activity

Not assessment instruments but accounts of what musical engagement consists of. Both of these were written partly against the narrowness of school music.

**Swanwick's C(L)A(S)P**  
*Keith Swanwick · A Basis for Music Education, 1979*  

Five modes of musical encounter, with three held to be direct and two supporting — the brackets are the argument, not a typographic accident.

C Composition direct (L) Literature studies supporting A Audition direct — listening as attentive encounter (S) Skill acquisition supporting P Performance direct

**Stance** Deliberately demotes technique and music history to supporting roles, against a tradition that made them the whole subject. Audition-as-a-direct-mode is close to your *Attending*.

**Elliott's praxial musicianship**  
*David Elliott · Music Matters, 1995*  

Musicianship as a multidimensional form of procedural knowledge, with formal, informal, impressionistic and supervisory knowledge surrounding a procedural core. Music understood as something people *do* — musicing — rather than a body of works.

**Stance** The philosophical companion to Small's musicking, and the most influential argument that knowing-how precedes and exceeds knowing-that in music.

*E*  

### Informal — how people actually learn music

Descriptive research on learning that happens without curricula, later converted into pedagogy. The family closest to your instincts, and the one that least resembles a competency framework.

**Green's informal learning model**  
*Lucy Green · Music, Informal Learning and the School, 2008 · Musical Futures*  

Five characteristics abstracted from how popular musicians actually learn, then deliberately imported into classrooms.

1 learners choose music they know, love and identify with 2 learning by ear from recordings, not from notation 3 peer learning in friendship groups 4 haphazard, personal, non-sequential progression 5 listening, composing, performing, improvising all at once

**Stance** One reported outcome is worth your attention: students said the process changed how they listened at home, many discovering "there was any more to music apart from the lyrics." That is your first aim, arrived at as a side effect and documented.

**Community music**  
*Higgins · Veblen · ISME CMA*  

A field defined by participation, hospitality and context rather than outcomes, working with groups that formal music education does not reach.

**Stance** Actively resists competency framing on the grounds that specifying outcomes in advance would undo the hospitality. The nearest thing in the literature to a principled defence of vague deliverables.

*F*  

### Cultural and civic competence

You raised this as related, and it is — but with one structural catch worth noticing up front.

**Reference Framework of Competences for Democratic Culture**  
*Council of Europe · 2016–18*  

Twenty competences across four categories, the most developed instrument of its kind, adopted across European education systems.

VALUES (3) human dignity and rights · cultural diversity · democracy, justice, fairness, equality, rule of law ATTITUDES (6) openness to cultural otherness · respect · civic-mindedness · responsibility · self-efficacy · tolerance of ambiguity SKILLS (8) autonomous learning · analytical and critical thinking · listening and observing · empathy · flexibility · linguistic and plurilingual · cooperation · conflict resolution KNOWLEDGE (3) critical understanding of self · of language and communication · of the world

**Stance** Includes *listening and observing* and *tolerance of ambiguity* as named competences — both of which a music workshop develops incidentally. Also demonstrates that dispositions *can* be put in a framework if the framework is willing to abandon short-term assessability.

**Culturally responsive teaching in music**  
*Lind & McKoy · Deardorff · Bennett's DMIS*  

Music-specific application of culturally responsive pedagogy, alongside the general intercultural competence models — Deardorff's process model and Bennett's developmental stages from denial to integration.

**Stance** **The catch:** almost all of this literature specifies the *teacher's* competence, not the learner's. If you want cultural competence as a learning aim rather than a facilitator qualification, you are largely on your own — and the RFCDC is the better borrow.

*G*  

### Clinical and critical — the two outside literatures

Neither is a music education framework, and two of your aims live almost entirely in them.

**Music therapy competencies**  
*AMTA Professional Competencies · CBMT Scope of Practice*  

Detailed professional competency sets covering musical foundations, clinical foundations and therapy skills, with an advanced tier. Adjacent programmes such as Singing for Lung Health formalise breath work through group singing.

**Stance** Where "singing as breathing exercise" actually lives. Framed as clinical competence for practitioners and as health outcome for participants — never as musical learning.

**The critical literature on standards**  
*MayDay Group · Action, Criticism and Theory · Mullen*  

Sustained argument that the standards movement in music education serves neoliberal and neoconservative interests at once — linking music to workplace skills and measurable outputs while codifying Eurocentric epistemologies of musical value behind inclusive rhetoric.

**Stance** No framework contains its own critique, so one of your aims is covered only here. Proposed alternatives centre on dialogical, local, student-as-producer practice rather than on better benchmarks.

## Four findings

#### The gap is not in your thinking, it is between literatures

Your sixteen aims are scattered across at least seven bodies of work that barely cite one another: music education standards, psychometric musicality research, German empirical *Kompetenz* modelling, informal-learning research, civic and intercultural competence, music therapy, and the sociology of music. Each covers some of your list well. None covers even half. There is no framework that holds capability, critique and disposition together — which is a reasonable description of what MTILT would be, if you decided it were a framework at all.

#### The absences are load-bearing, not oversights

Six of your aims are missing because competency frameworks must produce evidence a third party can check within a school year. "Sings in the car" and "understands why their taste is not a personality trait" fail that test. The Council of Europe's framework shows dispositions *can* be written down — it names tolerance of ambiguity as a competence — but only by giving up on short-term assessment. That is the trade, and it is the one worth making deliberately rather than by default.

#### Two frameworks are worth borrowing from directly

The German KoMus model, because it refuses to separate perceiving music from contextualising it — one competence, not two, which is close to a formal statement of your position and is empirically validated. And the Gold-MSI, because "active musical engagement" is the only place in the entire field where a person who mostly listens, collects and attends is credited with a musical life. Québec's *Apprécier* is the third, and it is already yours by jurisdiction.

#### Cultural competence frameworks are about teachers, not learners

You raised cultural competency as related, and it is — but the music-specific literature almost entirely specifies what a culturally responsive *teacher* knows and does. If you want it as a learner aim, the Council of Europe's twenty competences are the better source, since they are written for the person learning rather than the person facilitating.

## Grooves, and different tools

#### A groove is a competency object, and an appropriation problem, at once

You noted patterns brought from one context to another. A groove is precisely a pattern that travels and keeps working — which makes it unusually well suited to being a learning object, because its portability is the thing being learned. It is also exactly where your 2019 argument applies: the transfer that makes a groove teachable is the same transfer that makes appropriation a live question. A session built on one groove moving between contexts would carry a capability aim and a *Situating* aim in the same activity, which is otherwise hard to arrange. Of everything on your list, this is the item most likely to join the two halves.

#### Tools for the other kinds are collection tools, not instruments

You said tools might still help if appropriated well, and would be different tools. The survey supports that fairly specifically. The aims that instruments serve are the ones already covered by existing frameworks. The absent aims — taste and social group, commodification, diversity, narrowness — are served by *corpora*: things that let a group gather, compare, annotate and argue about material. Which is your information-architecture instinct pointed at a pedagogical purpose rather than a personal library, and it is a different build from an AUv3 plug-in.
