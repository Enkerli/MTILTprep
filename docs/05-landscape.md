# Maps We Won't Follow

Competency frameworks as surveys of territory rather than as routes to walk — and the terrain they describe, including the two ditches nobody puts on a map.

*The stance*

## There is no contradiction, and heutagogy has the word for it

Referring to competency frameworks while pursuing something open-ended only looks dissonant if you assume frameworks must be obeyed. Appropriation is the operative move here — the same one your 2019 article describes for technology. A framework is *matériel*: subvert it, curate it, connect it to another, critique it, build an alternative from its parts. Bricolage does not ask permission of the manufacturer.

The self-determined learning literature supplies a sharper term for the actual difference. **Competency is performance in known contexts; capability is what a person does in unfamiliar ones.** Frameworks specify the first because it can be evidenced. MTILT is after the second, which is why it keeps sliding out of their grasp — and heutagogy's whole point is that capability can be pursued deliberately without pre-specifying where the learner will end up. That is not a gap in your thinking. It is a named position with a literature behind it.

*Four ways of moving*  

## Your verbs, as modes of travel

Not competencies — a competency says what you should be able to do on arrival. These say how you are moving while you are still out there.

Scoutingreconnaissance

Going ahead to see what is there, with no obligation to bring anything back. Knowing what's out there is itself the aim — which is the honest reason to read framework documents at all, and the reason the reference package exists.

**In a session** Nobody is assessed on what they found. The report is "here's what I ran into", not "here's what I learned". **Risk** Scouting can become collecting. A survey that never turns into a walk is a library, not a journey. Decolonizingun-defaulting

Finding the unmarked defaults and marking them. The staff, the keyboard, 12TET, the target patch, the word "sophistication" — none forbidden, all demoted from *the* way to *a* way. This is where pitch-ontology's refusal of unmarked defaults becomes a mode of travel rather than a data policy.

**In a session** Show the default doing its work, then swap it and let people feel what changed. The swap teaches more than the critique. **Risk** Substituting one default for another. A ring is not more neutral than a grid — it hides different things. Enquiringasking properly

Treating a musical question as answerable by investigation rather than by authority. Where does this "rule" come from, who does it serve, what happens if we break it, what did somebody actually measure? The ethnographic mode, applied to music's own claims about itself.

**In a session** The facilitator does not know the answer either, and that is visible rather than concealed. **Risk** This is the mode that digs the first ditch. Enquiry is exactly what can disenchant. Playingthe point

Both senses at once, deliberately: playing music and playing around. Jamming, being a good hang, no guilty pleasures. The mode that makes the other three survivable, and the one most likely to be quietly dropped when a session starts feeling like a course.

**In a session** If nobody laughed and nothing was pointless, it was a class. **Risk** Play is the first thing sacrificed to legitimacy when an institution asks what the outcomes were. 
*The two ditches*

## What the maps don't mark

*Ditch · studied little, felt often*  

#### Disenchantment — learning too much about what you love

The realisation that arrives after analysis and cannot be un-had. The song turns out to be four chords you now recognise everywhere; the production trick you admired is a preset. And the listening itself changes — training installs an attention that is *selective and hierarchising*, always sorting foreground from background, always naming.

Your question was whether Deep Listening is a remedy. Probably yes, and for a specific reason rather than a spiritual one. Oliveros separates hearing, which is involuntary, from listening, which is voluntary — and then defines her practice as listening "in every possible way to everything possible." That is *inclusive* attention, the opposite mode from the selective kind that analysis trains. It does not un-learn anything. It adds a second gear, and the gear is deliberately not the analytical one.

> **The bridge** Name the ditch out loud before anyone falls in — it is a normal feature of the terrain, not a sign that someone ruined music for themselves. Then pair every analytical session with a non-analytical one, not as a reward but as the other half of the practice. Schafer's exercises and Oliveros's sonic meditations are both built for this and neither requires equipment.

*Ditch · studied a great deal*  

#### When desire outruns skill

You said this one has been studied, and it has — most usefully as flow's challenge–skill balance. Above the diagonal, where what you want exceeds what you can do, the state is anxiety; below it, boredom. The popular version is the gap between your taste and your output, which is the same geometry felt from inside.

Your instruction was not to design around it but to take it on. That is the right call, because designing around it means lowering ambition, and lowering someone's ambition to keep them comfortable is its own insult.

> **The bridge** Lower the challenge without lowering the taste. This is the one place your tools do real pastoral work: PitchFold removes wrong notes while leaving judgement entirely intact, so a person's ear stays ahead of their hands without their hands producing something they'd wince at. The gap stays open — it is supposed to — but the ditch stops being a place you sit in.

*Peak · genuinely hard, and worth saying so*  

#### The climbs that are actually climbs

Expanding an improviser's language. Memorising specific songs. These take sustained, unglamorous work, and a landscape that pretends everything is a gentle slope is condescending in a way people detect immediately. Informality is not the claim that nothing is difficult.

> **The bridge** Mark the elevation honestly and let people choose the climb. Heutagogy handles this without contradiction: a self-determined learner can absolutely commit to something arduous. What they will not accept is being marched up it.

## Translation, and the profile that isn't a profile

#### Speaking the territory's language before leaving it

The change-leadership half. People who have occupied formal music education for decades will read an alternative as ignorance unless it demonstrates that it knows what it is departing from. The reference package is the instrument for this: being able to say "Québec's *Apprécier* already carries the sociocultural dimension, and KoMus binds perceiving to contextualising — here is where I go further" is a different conversation from "school music is narrow."

Deviation reads as informed or as uninformed, and the only thing that settles which is whether you can state the other position accurately first.

#### A trail log, not a learner profile

You're right that the before-and-after shouldn't be overstated, and right to bristle at "sophistication." The Gold-MSI widened the *range* of what counts as a musical life — genuinely valuable, and rare — while keeping the *ranking*. Worse, one of its subscales measures time and money spent on music, which is cultural capital operationalised as expenditure. A scale that ranks people by what they spent is measuring distinction and calling it sophistication.

The landscape version is a log rather than a level: where you came from, where you went, what you ran into, what you're curious about next. Two people's logs can be compared without either being further along, because they are not on the same path. Goals still get set — and deviating from your own goal is information, which is exactly what a score cannot record.

## Systems adopted, then quietly abandoned

You filed the acoustics observation under critique and called it minor. It may be less minor than that, because it and the enharmonic complaint are the same bug seen twice.

Music theory invokes the overtone series to ground scales and consonance, and then everything reverts to 12TET without the approximation ever being examined. The connection is made and left. Not wrong — but presented as a foundation when it is an analogy that was dropped partway.

> #### And the same move, one octave down
>
> C♯∆ = C♯ · E♯ · G♯ not C♯ · F · G♯
>
> Letter-name spelling is a functional system: a triad is a stack of thirds, and the spelling is what makes that visible. Respelling E♯ as F buys a moment's ease and costs the chord its readability — C♯ to F is a diminished fourth on the page, so the chord stops looking like what it is, and every voice-leading or functional reading downstream gets harder. The guitaristic habit names what is under the finger; the confusion arrives later and compounds.
>
> Same shape as the overtone case: adopt a system for its explanatory power, break it silently where it charges a fee. Which suggests a critical thread running under MTILT that is more specific than "music theory is narrow" — **incomplete systems presented as complete**, and the pedagogical damage of never saying where the seam is. Offered as a reading of your two examples; reject it if it over-fits.

#### Byproducts, and why that framing holds up

Interactive modules — Building-Blocks-style JavaScript, H5P, small standalone tools — made *together* rather than delivered. Calling them byproducts rather than products is not modesty; it is the only framing consistent with everything above. A product implies a specification, an audience and a finish line. A byproduct is evidence that something happened, and it can be abandoned without anybody having failed.

It also resolves the sustainability question that usually kills open-ended projects: byproducts accumulate whether or not the project ever declares itself finished, which is the same property that makes Manifold and patch-panel useful at every stage.

#### Inputs for models — the enharmonic case is a good one

The spellings that language models default to are a live example of a corpus deciding a pedagogy. If the weight of available text leans guitaristic, the model leans guitaristic, and learners meet "F" in a C♯ chord as though it were the normal answer. Material that demonstrates other approaches — and, more usefully, material that *contextualises* why each spelling exists and what it costs — is a small, real intervention. Not the goal.
