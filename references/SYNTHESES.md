# Syntheses

One entry per source in `mtilt.bib`. Keys match the BibTeX keys, so this file and
a Zotero library stay alignable.

**This file is committed; the documents themselves are not.** PDFs and web
archives are gitignored and live locally, in Zotero. What travels with the repo
is the metadata, the synthesis, and — the part that matters — an honest record of
how much of each source was actually consulted.

## Evidence levels

Every entry is marked. The levels are not about a source's quality; they are
about **mine**.

| Level | Means |
|---|---|
| `read` | I worked from the document's own text, at length |
| `extracted` | I worked from the document's own text via automated extraction — compressed, but the source's own words and structure |
| `landing` | Only a publisher page, abstract, project page or lab page. The framing is the publisher's, not the author's |
| `secondary` | Someone else's account of the work, not the work |
| `background` | **Not consulted. General knowledge, unverified this session.** Treat as a lead to check, not as a finding |

Roughly twenty of the forty-six are `background` — mostly books. That is the
honest state of a bibliography assembled in one session, and marking it is
cheaper than discovering it later.

# A. Curricular frameworks

### `nccas2014` — National Coalition for Core Arts Standards (2014), *National Core Arts Standards: Music*
**Level:** `extracted` — the official Definitions/EUs/EQs PDF.

**What it is.** The dominant Anglophone school-music framework, organised as four
artistic processes with eleven anchor standards beneath them, elaborated per
grade band and per ensemble type.

**The structure.** *Creating* — imagine, plan and make, evaluate and refine,
present. *Performing* — select, analyze, interpret, rehearse/evaluate/refine,
present. *Responding* — select, analyze, interpret, evaluate. *Connecting* — two
anchors only: relate personal experience to making (#10), and relate works to
varied contexts and daily life (#11).

**For MTILT.** *Responding* is a usable, well-elaborated account of attending —
better than anything MTILT has written for its own *Attending* aims. *Connecting*
is the only door to anything outside music and it is by far the thinnest of the
four, which is why "connections between music and other domains" scored Partial
in `docs/04`. The process-based design was the 2014 reform's achievement: it
moved the framework off repertoire and onto verbs.

### `meq2001arts` — Ministère de l'Éducation du Québec (2001), *Programme de formation de l'école québécoise: domaine des arts*
**Level:** `landing` — the RÉCIT arts service's competency pages, not the PFEQ chapter itself.

**What it is.** Three competencies, stated as verbs, identical in shape across
the four arts disciplines: **C1 Inventer**, **C2 Interpréter**, **C3 Apprécier**.

**Why it matters here more than its size suggests.** *Apprécier* is described as
carrying a **sociocultural dimension alongside the technical one** — appreciation
is not only aesthetic judgement. That makes Québec's third competency a better
hook for MTILT's *Situating* aims than anything in the American standards, and it
is already the operative framework in Alex's own jurisdiction.

**To verify.** Read `chapitre084v2.pdf` directly. The sociocultural claim comes
from RÉCIT's summary, and how much weight the PFEQ itself puts on it is exactly
the thing worth checking before leaning on it in a conversation with a Québec
music educator.

### `dfe2021mmc` — Department for Education (2021), *Model Music Curriculum*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** England's non-statutory model curriculum, sitting under the
statutory triad of performing, composing and listening, adding year-by-year
expectations and repertoire lists.

**For MTILT.** Its interest is as a case: the repertoire lists reopened the
argument about whose music counts that process-based reform had tried to settle.
A framework can move to verbs and still smuggle a canon back in through its
examples. Worth checking before citing — I have not read the document.

# B. Professional and higher education

### `aecpolifonia` — Association Européenne des Conservatoires, *AEC/Polifonia Learning Outcomes*
**Level:** `extracted` — the outcomes PDF, all three cycles.

**What it is.** The fullest articulation anywhere of what a trained musician is
supposed to be able to do, across the three Bologna cycles, in three groups:
**practical** (artistic expression, repertoire, ensemble, practising/rehearsing/
reading/aural/creative, verbal, public performance, improvisation, pedagogy),
**theoretical** (repertoire and materials, context, improvisation, pedagogical
knowledge), and **generic** (independence, psychological understanding, critical
awareness, communication).

**Three things worth stealing.** Improvisation is treated as a practical skill
*and* a body of theoretical knowledge — most frameworks pick one. "Verbal skills
— discuss music intelligently" sits on the same footing as playing, which is the
*Naming* aim, formalised. And the third cycle asks graduates to "question
conventions" and "challenge community standards", which is further toward
critique than any school framework goes.

**For MTILT.** This is the document to know when translating to formal music
people. Being able to say where MTILT departs from Polifonia, specifically, is
what makes the departure read as informed.

### Graded examination syllabi — ABRSM · Trinity · RSL
**Level:** `background`. Not in the bib as entries; noted here because `docs/04`
discusses them.

Eight or nine levels per instrument, each specifying repertoire, technical work,
sight-reading and aural tests. Functionally the most widely used competency
ladders in the world and rarely called that. RSL's popular-music grades are the
interesting case: they had to invent assessment criteria for improvisation, band
skills and production, which forced articulation of things classical ladders left
tacit. **Unverified** — worth a look if the ladder question ever matters.

# C. Psychometric models

### `mullensiefen2014goldmsi` — Müllensiefen, Gingras, Musil & Stewart (2014), *The Musicality of Non-Musicians*, PLoS ONE
**Level:** `landing` — the Goldsmiths lab page listing the subscales. Not the paper.

**What it is.** A self-report index of "musical sophistication" with five
subscales: **active musical engagement** (time and money spent on music),
**self-reported perceptual abilities**, **musical training**, **self-reported
singing abilities**, and **sophisticated emotional engagement** (ability to talk
about the emotions music expresses). Explicitly built to be sensitive to
differences *among* people who would not call themselves musicians.

**What is genuinely rare in it.** *Active musical engagement* is, as far as this
survey found, the only place in the literature where a person who mostly listens,
collects and attends is credited with a measurable musical life. That construct
is worth having.

**Why it is flagged anyway.** The construct name ranks. "Sophistication" is a
distinction word, and the index widened the *range* of what counts while keeping
the *ordering*. Worse, one subscale operationalises musical life as **time and
money spent** — cultural capital measured as expenditure. A scale that ranks
people by outlay is measuring distinction and calling it sophistication.

**For MTILT.** Keep the idea, leave the scale. The curation-in-passes model in
Alex's own repos (`docs/06`) is the alternative, and it predates the objection.

### `jordan2012komus` — Jordan, Knigge, Lehmann, Niessen & Lehmann-Wermser (2012), *Entwicklung und Validierung eines Kompetenzmodells im Fach Musik*
**Level:** `landing` — abstracts and the Universität Bremen project page. The German papers were not read.

**What it is.** An empirically developed and validated competence model for school
music, built with the psychometric seriousness German education research applies
to every subject. The core competence is named as a pair: **"Musik wahrnehmen und
kontextualisieren"** — perceiving *and* contextualising music, as one competence.
`KOPRA-M` extends the approach to practical musical competences.

**Why the pairing is the point.** Most frameworks let you perceive without
contextualising, and treat context as separate content. This one refuses the
split. That is close to a formal, validated statement of MTILT's own position —
and it is the strongest single borrow available.

**To verify.** The model's internal levels and how the pairing is operationalised
in test items. Both are in German-language papers not read here.

### Aptitude and perception batteries — Gordon · PROMS · MET
**Level:** `background`. Noted in `docs/04`, not in the bib.

Audiation-based aptitude testing and perceptual discrimination batteries. Treat
musical ability as something a person *has an amount of* — the frame MTILT is
most directly opposed to. Listed so the opposition is explicit rather than
implied.

# D. Theoretical models of musical activity

### `swanwick1979basis` — Keith Swanwick (1979), *A Basis for Music Education*
**Level:** `background`. The model is well known; the book was not consulted.

**What it is.** C(L)A(S)P: **C**omposition, **L**iterature studies,
**A**udition, **S**kill acquisition, **P**erformance — with the brackets marking
an argument, not a typographic choice. Composition, Audition and Performance are
held to be direct modes of musical encounter; Literature and Skill acquisition
are supporting.

**For MTILT.** Written against a tradition that had made technique and music
history the whole subject, and it demotes both. *Audition as a direct mode* — as
a form of musical engagement in its own right, not preparation for one — is very
close to MTILT's *Attending*.

**Unverified.** The expansion above and the direct/supporting split are from
general knowledge. Check against the book before quoting.

### `elliott1995matters` — David Elliott (1995), *Music Matters*
**Level:** `background`, plus the author's own site headings.

**What it is.** The praxial philosophy of music education. Musicianship is a
multidimensional form of **procedural knowledge** — knowing-how, surrounded by
formal, informal, impressionistic and supervisory knowledge. Music is understood
as something people *do* (musicing) rather than as a body of works.

**For MTILT.** The philosophical companion to Small, and the most influential
argument that knowing-how precedes and exceeds knowing-that in music. Useful when
the *Naming* aim needs defending as secondary rather than primary.

**Unverified.** The five knowledge dimensions are from general knowledge.

### `small1998musicking` — Christopher Small (1998), *Musicking*
**Level:** `background`. Cited in Enkerli 2019, which I did read.

**What it is.** Music as a verb. Every act contributing to a musical performance
— playing, listening, rehearsing, taking tickets, sweeping the hall afterwards —
is musicking. The meaning is in the relationships the performance enacts, not in
an object.

**For MTILT.** The reason "casual musicking as liberating" is a coherent aim, and
the reason it resists competency framing by construction: musicking has no
achievement condition. Also, via Rouget's *musiquer*, the frame the 2019 article
already uses.

# E. Informal and self-determined learning

### `green2008informal` — Lucy Green (2008), *Music, Informal Learning and the School*
**Level:** `secondary` — the Open University's account of the model. Not Green's text.

**What it is.** Five characteristics abstracted from how popular musicians
actually learn, then deliberately imported into classrooms as a pedagogy (the
basis of Musical Futures): learners choose music they **know, love and identify
with**; they learn **by ear from recordings**, not from notation; they work in
**friendship groups**; progression is **haphazard and personal** rather than
sequential; and listening, composing, performing and improvising develop
**simultaneously** rather than in stages.

**The finding worth having.** Students reported the process changed how they
listened at home — many discovering, in the account's phrasing, that "there was
any more to music apart from the lyrics." That is MTILT's first aim (*unpacking
what they like, noticing what makes it work*) arriving as a **side effect** of
learner-chosen material, and documented rather than hoped for.

**The teacher's role inverts.** Educators "stand back", observe what students are
trying to do, and offer targeted help — which is the heutagogical posture, in a
classroom, a decade before MTILT wrote it down.

**To verify.** Green's own account of what the model costs. Secondary summaries
are uniformly positive; the critical literature on it (tensions, issues and
strengths) exists and was not read.

### `higgins2012community` — Lee Higgins (2012), *Community Music: In Theory and in Practice*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** Community music as a field defined by participation, hospitality
and context rather than by outcomes, working with groups formal music education
does not reach.

**For MTILT.** The nearest thing in the literature to a **principled defence of
vague deliverables**: specifying outcomes in advance would undo the hospitality
that makes the practice work. If MTILT ever has to justify its openness to an
institution, this is the tradition to stand in.

**Unverified.** "Hospitality" as Higgins's organising term is from general
knowledge (it draws on Derrida). Check before leaning on it.

### `hase2013heutagogy` — Hase & Kenyon, eds. (2013), *Self-Determined Learning: Heutagogy in Action*
**Level:** `landing` — review articles and the EdTechBooks chapter page. The book was not read.

**What it is.** Heutagogy as the third term after pedagogy and andragogy: learning
that is **self-determined**, where the learner sets not only the pace but the
questions, the path and the criteria.

**The distinction that resolves MTILT's apparent contradiction.**
**Competency is performance in known contexts; capability is what a person does
in unfamiliar ones.** Frameworks specify competency because it can be evidenced.
MTILT is after capability — which is why it kept sliding out of their grasp, and
why the slide is not a failure of rigour. Associated ideas: learner-defined
learning contracts, and **double-loop learning** (questioning the assumptions
behind an action, not only the action).

**For MTILT.** This is the single most useful vocabulary borrow in the whole
bibliography. It gives a defensible name to the position and a literature to
point at.

**To verify.** The competency/capability formulation above is consistently
reported in the secondary literature but was not read in Hase & Kenyon's own
words. Worth pinning to a page number before using it in an argument.

### `blum2020ungrading` — Susan D. Blum, ed. (2020), *Ungrading*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** An edited collection on why rating students undermines learning,
and what people do instead — contract grading, portfolios, self-assessment,
narrative feedback.

**For MTILT.** Directly relevant to the refusal of scoreboards, and a source of
practical alternatives rather than only critique. Note the tension worth keeping:
ungrading literature mostly assumes a course with a transcript at the end. MTILT
has neither, which makes it easier and also means the literature's hardest
problem is not MTILT's problem.

# F. Cultural and civic competence

### `coe2018rfcdc` — Council of Europe (2018), *Reference Framework of Competences for Democratic Culture*
**Level:** `extracted` — the twenty-competence handout, in full.

**What it is.** Twenty competences in four groups. **Values (3):** human dignity
and human rights; cultural diversity; democracy, justice, fairness, equality and
the rule of law. **Attitudes (6):** openness to cultural otherness; respect;
civic-mindedness; responsibility; self-efficacy; tolerance of ambiguity.
**Skills (8):** autonomous learning; analytical and critical thinking; listening
and observing; empathy; flexibility and adaptability; linguistic, communicative
and plurilingual skills; cooperation; conflict resolution. **Knowledge and
critical understanding (3):** of the self; of language and communication; of the
world.

**Two reasons it is the better borrow.** It is written for the **learner**, not
for the teacher — unlike almost all the music-specific cultural competence
literature. And it demonstrates that **dispositions can be put in a framework**:
*tolerance of ambiguity* is competence #9. The price is that they gave up
short-term assessability to do it, which is exactly the trade MTILT should make
deliberately rather than by default.

**Incidentally useful.** *Listening and observing* and *tolerance of ambiguity*
are both things a music workshop develops without trying. That is a translation
bridge to any institution that has adopted the RFCDC.

### `lind2016culturally` — Lind & McKoy (2016), *Culturally Responsive Teaching in Music Education*
**Level:** `background`, plus publisher pages.

**What it is.** The music-specific application of culturally responsive pedagogy:
how a teacher's practice can meet students whose musical cultures differ from the
school's.

**The catch, and it is structural.** This literature specifies the **teacher's**
competence, not the learner's. If cultural competence is wanted as a *learning
aim* rather than as a facilitator qualification, this is the wrong shape and the
RFCDC is the right one. Worth knowing before recommending it to anyone.

### `deardorff2006identification` — Darla Deardorff (2006), *Identification and Assessment of Intercultural Competence*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** The process model of intercultural competence — attitudes feeding
knowledge and skills, feeding internal outcomes (frame of reference shift),
feeding external outcomes (behaviour). Derived by Delphi study among
interculturalists, which is why it is widely cited as *the* consensus model.

**For MTILT.** Interesting mainly for its ordering: **attitudes come first**.
That is the ASK inversion from the notes, arrived at independently in another
field.

# G. Clinical

### `amtacompetencies` — American Music Therapy Association, *Professional Competencies*
**Level:** `landing` — the competencies page.

**What it is.** A professional competency set covering musical foundations,
clinical foundations and music therapy skills, with an advanced tier; paired with
the CBMT Scope of Practice.

**For MTILT.** This is where "singing as breathing exercise" actually lives —
framed as clinical competence for practitioners and health outcome for
participants, never as musical learning. Adjacent programmes (Singing for Lung
Health) formalise group singing as breath work. The relevance is the **framing
gap**: nobody treats breath as the musical point rather than the means.

# H. Critical literature

### `mullen2019standards` — Jennifer Mullen (2019), *Music Education for Some*, ACT 18(1)
**Level:** `extracted` — the full article PDF.

**What it is.** An argument that the National Core Arts Standards represent an
alliance of neoliberal and neoconservative interests that **exacerbates**
inequity rather than remedying it.

**The two halves.** *Neoliberal*: standards link music to workplace skills and
human capital, establish uniform benchmarks prioritising efficiency and
measurable outcomes, intensify administrative load and deskill teachers, and
create markets for assessment products. *Neoconservative*: despite inclusive
rhetoric, they codify **Eurocentric epistemologies of musical value**, with model
assessments privileging Western classical traditions — preserving dominant
cultural knowledge while appearing diverse.

**The alternative proposed.** Democratic music education: dialogical relations
("teacher-students and student-teachers", after Freire), local and contextual
learning responding to community traditions and youth culture, students as
cultural producers rather than consumers, collaborative creation instead of
benchmarks.

**For MTILT.** This is where the aim "understanding the issues with how narrow
musical learning tends to be" actually lives — no framework contains its own
critique. Also a caution: the alternative Mullen proposes is close enough to
MTILT that its weaknesses are probably MTILT's too.

### `attali1977bruits` — Jacques Attali (1977), *Bruits*
**Level:** `background`. Cited in Enkerli 2019, which I did read.

**What it is.** Music as political economy, and as prophecy — Attali's claim is
that changes in how music is produced and circulated precede analogous changes in
the wider economy. The historical stages (sacrificing, representing, repeating,
composing) organise the argument.

**For MTILT.** The commodification aim has no home in music education frameworks
and this is the canonical text for it. The 2019 article already uses Attali for
the point that intellectual property grants sound a materiality it does not
otherwise have.

# I. Representation and notation

### `seeger1958prescriptive` — Charles Seeger (1958), *Prescriptive and Descriptive Music-Writing*, *The Musical Quarterly* XLIV(2)
**Level:** `read` — the full article. The JSTOR scan carries a shifted font encoding; the text was recovered by applying a uniform +29 offset before reading.

**What it actually says.** The paper opens not with the famous distinction but
with **three hazards inherent in our practices of writing music**, and the
distinction is only the third:

1. **The assumption that the full auditory parameter of music is, or can be,
   represented by a partial visual parameter** — "one with only two dimensions
   upon a flat surface."
2. **Ignoring the historical lag of music-writing behind speech-writing**, and the
   consequent traditional interposition of the art of speech in the matching of
   auditory and visual signals in music writing.
3. **Failing to distinguish prescriptive from descriptive uses of music writing** —
   "between a blueprint of how a specific piece of music shall be made to sound
   and a report of how a specific performance of it actually did sound."

**The first hazard is the one MTILT had been under-using.** It is the whole
representation argument in a sentence, from 1958: a two-dimensional visual surface
is being asked to stand for the full auditory parameter, and the asking is
invisible. Everything in `docs/02` about what each channel makes disappear is a
worked-out version of this.

**The question he poses.** Whether speech conceptions of melody correspond to our
*music* conceptions of it — and "to what extent does the visual representation of
melody condition both conceptions of it?" That is the pianocentrism complaint and
the staff complaint stated as a research question rather than as a grievance, and
Seeger takes it seriously enough to treat speech-conceptions of melody as having
shaped not only notation but composition and performance themselves.

**Scope, worth knowing before citing.** The article deals with "only the simplest
kind of music: unaccompanied melody." The argument generalises, but Seeger did not
generalise it here.

**For MTILT.** Still the first thing to read, and now for a better reason than the
prescriptive/descriptive slogan. Hazard one is the project's own thesis with
sixty-eight years of seniority.

### `wiggins1993framework` — Wiggins, Miranda, Smaill & Harris (1993), *A Framework for the Evaluation of Music Representation Systems*, *Computer Music Journal* 17(3), pp. 31–42
**Level:** `read` — the full article. **Previously flagged; now verified.**

**The flag, resolved.** This entry used to record that the axes attributed to this
paper elsewhere in the repo came from my own search query and were unverified. The
PDF is now in hand and **they are the paper's own terms**, defined on its second
page:

> We consider the relative merits of different systems along two orthogonal
> dimensions: **expressive completeness** and **structural generality**. Expressive
> completeness refers to the range of raw musical data that can be represented, and
> structural generality refers to the range of high-level structures that can be
> represented and manipulated.

The wrong-until-checked note is kept above rather than deleted, per the GAPS.md
convention: the correction is worth less than the record of how it happened.

**What it is.** A two-axis scheme for placing representation systems relative to
each other rather than ranking them, oriented to three task types — **recording**
(accuracy is the concern), **analysis** (finding and exploiting structure), and
**generation/composition** (manipulability and flexibility). Systems are plotted
in a single figure against the two axes.

**The worked examples that matter here.**

- **Waveform** — maximal expressive completeness, minimal structural generality:
  captures any particular performance at the cost of abstract musical content, so
  "a very similar performance of the same piece may look very different."
- **MIDI** — potentially high on completeness in *time* (as close to what was
  played as the clock ticks allow) but low overall, because pitch is approximated
  to piano keys. And the line worth quoting at length: *"No explicit assumption is
  made about tuning systems; equal or just temperament use the same note
  representation in a MIDI file, but the representation does not encompass both;
  it acknowledges neither, simply ignoring the issue."* Structurally, MIDI "scores
  badly" — no allowance for structural annotation below the level of a whole file.
- **Traditional score notation** — more structural generality than MIDI (it gives
  tonal and metric information) but "restricted in expressive completeness to
  traditional Western tonal music."
- **DARMS** — occupies *exactly the same place in the figure as the score itself*,
  because it encodes scores as uninterpreted graphic symbols: a curved line
  "denotes neither a phrase nor a slur."
- **UPIC** — scores well on expressive completeness thanks to the granularity and
  flexibility of its basic event, but is "hardly hierarchical at all" at the level
  of notes: pages are stored separately and manipulated like images in a simple
  WYSIWYG program.

**For MTILT.** Three payoffs. The MIDI tuning passage is pitch-ontology's thesis —
that MIDI is a representational technology rather than a neutral primitive —
stated in 1993 by computer musicians, and it is a ready citation for the tuning
work. The UPIC analysis places DrawnQurve's ancestor precisely and names its
weakness (flat structure), which is a design note for any drawn-gesture tool. And
the framework itself is orthogonal to Seeger's: Seeger asks *what is a notation
for*, Wiggins et al. ask *what can it hold*. Together they are a usable pair of
questions for any representation in `docs/02`.

**Caveat.** The paper restricts itself to systems working "on the level of notes
and more abstract structures." Signal-level and morphological representations are
outside its scope by design, so it cannot adjudicate the timbre question.

### `selfridgefield1997beyondmidi` — Eleanor Selfridge-Field, ed. (1997), *Beyond MIDI: The Handbook of Musical Codes*
**Level:** `landing` — front matter and table of contents only (the local PDF is ten pages of prelims, not the book).

**What the contents page shows.** Part 1 is "Describing Musical Information",
opening with §1.1 *What Is Musical Representation?* and §1.2 *Parameters of
Musical Information* — so the book begins with the same question `docs/02` asks,
before descending into the code-by-code survey.

**What it is.** A code-by-code handbook of symbolic music representations, each
described by somebody who worked with it. Pre-dates most of the web and remains
the fullest single inventory of the field.

**For MTILT.** Reference rather than reading. The value is that it treats a
representation as a **designed artefact with trade-offs**, which is the stance
`docs/02` takes for a wider set of channels.

### `tenorconference` — TENOR, *International Conference on Technologies for Music Notation and Representation*
**Level:** `landing` — the proceedings index.

**What it is.** A conference series running since 2015, with open proceedings,
devoted entirely to notation and representation as a research question.

**For MTILT.** The community already arguing about exactly this. If the
representation thread ever wants an audience, it is here — and the proceedings
are a corpus of attempted answers to "how do you notate the thing that has no
notation."

### `skold2020notation` — Mattias Sköld (2020), *The Notation of Sound for Composition and Transcription*, TENOR
**Level:** `extracted` — the full paper.

**What it is.** A notation system for electroacoustic music, built on Thoresen's
spectromorphological analysis but extended for **composing** rather than only
analysing.

**The argument.** Thoresen and Hedman's symbols describe heard sounds
excellently, but analytical notation is not compositional notation: composition
must communicate a work for performance, not report what exists. So the spectral
analysis symbols are translated into actionable acoustic properties, on a hybrid
**pitch-frequency staff** carrying three spectrum categories (pitched, dystonic,
complex), spectral width, density and centroid, energy articulation (granularity,
modulation, amplitude envelopes), and 2D/3D spatialisation.

**The lineage it documents.** Schaeffer's typomorphology (~28 categories) →
Thoresen's streamlined version (15) → Sköld's compositional adaptation.

**For MTILT, with the caveat.** This is the most developed timbre-notation work
found, and it does **not** contradict Alex's position that timbre representation
is rare *in musical learning*. It is analytical and compositional apparatus, and
its cost is a symbol set to be learned — which sits awkwardly against bypassing
terminology.

### `thoresen2007spectromorphological` — Thoresen with Hedman (2007), *Organised Sound* 12(2)
**Level:** `secondary` — Sköld's account of it, plus search snippets. Open copies exist (CCCM, EMS Network) and are in the fetch script.

**What it is.** The paper that turned Schaeffer's typomorphology into a usable
analytical notation: roughly 28 categories reduced to 15, with a working symbol
set, under the Aural Sonology programme at the Norwegian Academy of Music.

**For MTILT.** The bridge between Schaeffer and anything teachable. Worth reading
properly before deciding whether the timbre track goes through symbols at all —
this is the strongest case for "yes", and reading it is how to test that.

### `schaeffer1966traite` — Pierre Schaeffer (1966), *Traité des objets musicaux*
**Level:** `background`. Referenced in Enkerli 2019.

**What it is.** The foundational text of musique concrète's theory: **reduced
listening** (écoute réduite — attending to the sound itself, severed from its
source), the **sound object**, and the typomorphology that classifies them.

**For MTILT.** Reduced listening is precisely the descent in `docs/03`: keep
removing context until the sound stops being a recording *of* something. And the
single-cycle hinge is where a sound object stops being a note. Worth noting that
Schaeffer got there from an aesthetics, not a pedagogy — the teaching version has
to be built.

### `toussaint2013geometry` — Godfried Toussaint (2013), *The Geometry of Musical Rhythm*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** Rhythm as geometry: patterns as necklaces on a circle, Euclidean
rhythms via Bjorklund's algorithm, maximal evenness, and distance measures
between patterns.

**For MTILT.** Underpins the ring representations in Alex's own tools and the
rotational-equivalence material in `docs/01`. The mathematics crossing that is
genuinely one object rather than an illustration.

**Unverified.** Publication details and the extent of the necklace framing are
from general knowledge.

### `weibel2020upic` — Weibel, Brümmer & Kanach, eds. (2020), *From Xenakis's UPIC to Graphic Notation Today*, ZKM
**Level:** `landing` — the ZKM and archive.org pages. Open access.

**What it is.** A large open-access volume on UPIC — Xenakis's 1977 drawing table
wired to a synthesis engine — and its descendants in graphic notation.

**For MTILT.** DrawnQurve's ancestry, documented. Draw-to-sound is not a novelty
interaction; it is a fifty-year line with a literature, and saying so places the
tool rather than isolating it.

# J. Timbre and sound design

### `saitis2019semantics` — Saitis & Weinzierl (2019), *The Semantics of Timbre*
**Level:** `extracted` — the chapter PDF.

**What it is.** The survey chapter on how people put timbre into words, and what
those words correlate with acoustically.

**The three recurring dimensions.** **Brightness / luminance / sharpness** —
tracks spectral centroid; shifts in the spectral envelope are heard systematically
as brightness changes. **Roughness / harshness / texture** — tracks amplitude
modulation within a critical band (roughly 15–300 Hz) plus high-frequency energy.
**Fullness / richness / mass** — tracks fundamental strength, inharmonicity and
spectral variation over time.

**The cross-linguistic finding.** Zacharakis et al., comparing English and Greek
speakers, found native language had very little effect on the configuration of the
semantic space — though some divergence exists (the Greek equivalent of "thick"
loads onto luminance rather than mass). So these three are close to a floor, not
a Western convention being imported.

**The instabilities, which are workshop material.** Musicians rate more
consistently than non-musicians. Semantic-differential method assumes true
antonym pairs (dark–bright) and those relationships are problematic. Words shift
across contexts — "soft" describes both loudness and transient character. Some
descriptors are instrument-family specific (trombone attracts emotion words other
brass do not). And the known gap: **semantic attributes fail at the
sustained-versus-impulsive distinction**, one of the biggest perceptual
dimensions. There is a hole in language exactly where a session could point.

**For MTILT.** Vindicates the Farnell-derived distinction: *bright* and *hollow*
have acoustic correlates and cross-linguistic stability; *cinematic* has neither.

### `wallmark2019crosstalk` — Zachary Wallmark (2019), *Semantic Crosstalk in Timbre Perception*, Music & Science
**Level:** `background` — search results only. Fully open access; now in the fetch script.

**What it is.** On interference between semantic dimensions in timbre perception —
how a judgement on one attribute contaminates a judgement on another.

**For MTILT.** Likely the best single source on *why* descriptor tasks go sideways
in a room, which is the practical half of the timbre-descriptor session. Unread;
high priority.

### `reymore2020timbretrait` — Lindsey Reymore (2020), *Timbre Trait Analysis*, Timbre 2020
**Level:** `background` — title and venue only. Open PDF, in the fetch script.

**What it is.** Reymore's work derives imagery-based dimensions of timbre qualia
and applies them to instrumentation.

**For MTILT.** The imagery framing — what a timbre makes you *picture* — may be
closer to how participants actually talk than the semantic-differential
tradition. Unread; worth checking before designing the descriptor session.

### `farnell2010designing` — Andy Farnell (2010), *Designing Sound*
**Level:** `background`. Cited in Enkerli 2019.

**What it is.** Procedural audio: synthesising sound from models of how it is
physically produced, rather than from recordings. Pure Data throughout.

**For MTILT.** Two things. It is the source of the scepticism about unstable
descriptors. And procedural audio is itself a pedagogy — building a sound from a
model of its cause teaches what makes it that sound, which is the ascent in
`docs/03` done in synthesis rather than in listening.

# K. Listening practices, play, and the ditches

### `schafer1967earcleaning` / `schafer1992soundeducation` — R. Murray Schafer
**Level:** `landing` — Schafer 360 (uOttawa) and catalogue pages.

**What they are.** *Ear Cleaning* (1967) is notes for an experimental music course
built on heightening awareness of surrounding sound. *A Sound Education* (1992) is
100 exercises in listening and sound-making. With *The Composer in the Classroom*
and *Creative Music Education*, they form a practical body aimed at teachers.

**The principle.** Schafer grounds instruction in **sounds students already
encounter daily**, rather than starting from theory. The acoustic world is the
curriculum's foundation.

**For MTILT.** This is the half-remembered approach, and it is what it seemed to
be. *A Sound Education* is probably the single most directly usable book in this
bibliography — a hundred exercises requiring nothing.

### `oliveros2005deep` — Pauline Oliveros (2005), *Deep Listening: A Composer's Sound Practice*
**Level:** `landing` — the Center for Deep Listening, RPI.

**What it is.** Deep Listening as "a way of listening in every possible way to
everything possible, to hear no matter what you are doing" — a lifelong practice
comprising bodywork, sonic meditations and interactive performance, extending to
sounds of daily life, nature, thought, imagination and dreams.

**The distinction that does the work.** **Hearing is involuntary; listening is
voluntary and selective.**

**For MTILT — the answer to "is it a remedy?"** Probably yes, for a mechanical
reason rather than a spiritual one. Analytical training installs *selective,
hierarchising* attention: always sorting foreground from background, always
naming. Deep Listening is *inclusive* attention — the opposite mode. It does not
un-learn anything; it adds a second gear, and the gear is deliberately not the
analytical one. Which makes it pairable with analysis rather than a retreat from
it.

### `csikszentmihalyi1990flow` — Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi (1990), *Flow*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** Optimal experience as a state arising when challenge and skill are
both high and in balance. Above the diagonal, where challenge exceeds skill, the
state is **anxiety**; below it, **boredom**.

**For MTILT.** The studied form of "when skills don't match desires and goals".
The design consequence in `docs/05`: lower the challenge without lowering the
taste, which is what PitchFold does — removes wrong notes while leaving judgement
intact.

**Unverified.** The channel model is general knowledge; the musician-specific
flow literature was not surveyed.

### `huizinga1938homoludens` — Johan Huizinga (1938), *Homo Ludens*
**Level:** `background`. Cited in Enkerli 2019.

**What it is.** Play as culture-generating and serious — prior to culture rather
than a product of it, with the magic circle marking play's separate space and
time.

**For MTILT.** The ground under "playfulness" as a stance rather than a
technique, and the counterweight to any reading of play as trivial.

### `caillois1958jeux` — Roger Caillois (1958), *Les jeux et les hommes*
**Level:** `landing` — Wikipedia and a review article listing. The book was not read.

**What it is.** Written partly in response to Huizinga. Four categories — *agôn*
(competition), *alea* (chance), *mimicry* (simulation), *ilinx* (vertigo) — cut
across a continuum from **paidia** (free, tumultuous, improvised play) to
**ludus** (play bound by convention and arbitrary difficulty).

**For MTILT — the toys-not-games note, formalised.** Gamification imports ludus
mechanics (points, levels, streaks, leaderboards) and loses paidia entirely,
which is exactly "game mechanics without the fun of play". The design question
that falls out: **does the thing have a win condition?** If it does, the
scoreboard is back. Will Wright's calling SimCity a *software toy* is the same
observation from industry.

### `oldenburg1989greatgoodplace` — Ray Oldenburg (1989), *The Great Good Place*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** The **third place** — neither home (first) nor work (second):
neutral ground, a leveller, conversation as the main activity, accessible and
accommodating, with regulars, a low profile, a playful mood, and the feel of a
home away from home.

**For MTILT.** "Being a good hang" is close to a definition of third-place
behaviour, and a hackspace is a third place with tools. This is probably the
**venue answer** for MTILT sessions, which so far have had no venue.

### `neff2011selfcompassion` — Kristin Neff (2011), *Self-Compassion*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** Self-compassion as three components: **self-kindness** (rather
than self-judgement), **common humanity** (recognising difficulty as shared
rather than as personal deficiency), and **mindfulness** (holding painful feeling
in balanced awareness).

**For MTILT.** *Common humanity* is the active ingredient for the desire/skill
ditch — and it is the same move as "acknowledging what'll be difficult". Saying a
climb is steep before anyone starts is a compassion practice, not just honesty.

# L. The crossings

### `godoy2010musicalgestures` — Godøy & Leman, eds. (2010), *Musical Gestures: Sound, Movement, and Meaning*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** The collection on music as embodied: sound-producing and
sound-accompanying gesture, motor imagery in listening, and the phenomenological
line running through Merleau-Ponty.

**For MTILT.** The embodiment crossing, and the most underexploited one given how
gestural the suite already is — DrawnQurve, breath, Vane. Also the theoretical
backing for "music and dance" as one object rather than two subjects.

### `xenakis1992formalized` — Iannis Xenakis (1992), *Formalized Music*, 2nd ed.
**Level:** `background`, plus the Xenakis foundation's pages on the Philips Pavilion.

**What it is.** Xenakis's own account of stochastic, symbolic and set-theoretic
composition. The biographical fact that matters here is separate: he trained and
worked as an architect with Le Corbusier.

**The crossing, and why it is the cleanest example.** The Philips Pavilion
(Brussels, 1958) and *Metastaseis* share a generating idea — string glissandi and
ruled surfaces are the same geometry, realised once in sound and once in concrete.
Not music illustrated by architecture: **one object, built twice.** That is the
test for when a crossing is real, with a worked case attached.

**Unverified.** The pavilion/Metastaseis relationship is well documented but was
confirmed here only from search results and the foundation's page titles.

### `scott2010tamildaa` — Alan P. Scott, *Talking About Music Is Like Dancing About Architecture*
**Level:** `landing` — search results. The page is in the archive list; it was not fetched.

**What it is.** The catalogue of attributions for the aphorism — Martin Mull,
Elvis Costello, Steve Martin, Laurie Anderson, Zappa, Monk — maintained at a URL
that is literally `tamildaa.htm`, which is where the acronym comes from. Cited as
Scott 2010 in Enkerli 2019.

**For MTILT.** The unresolved attribution is part of how the put-down works:
nobody has to own it. And the dismissal has a flaw worth exploiting — it only
stings if translation must be lossless, which is precisely the assumption
`docs/02` spends its length dismantling. Dancing about architecture is a
perfectly good thing to do; it is site-specific dance, and it exists.

### `nime` — *International Conference on New Interfaces for Musical Expression*
**Level:** `landing` — the conference site.

**What it is.** Running since 2001 (out of a CHI workshop), open proceedings. The
venue where instrument design is treated as a research question in its own right.

**For MTILT.** Where the "more intuitive than most instruments" aim has a
literature — and the one aim in `docs/04` that scored Absent because no competency
framework touches instrument design. NIME is the answer to that absence.

### `kaplan1964lawinstrument` — Abraham Kaplan (1964), *The Conduct of Inquiry*
**Level:** `background`.

**What it is.** Source of the law of the instrument: give a small boy a hammer and
he will find that everything he encounters needs pounding. Maslow's better-known
1966 version followed.

**For MTILT.** The precise form of the hex-grid note: a hex grid **is** as much a
Golden Hammer as a piano. The value was never neutrality — it was having more than
one hammer and knowing each one's grip.

# M. The project's own ground

### `enkerli2019son` — Alexandre Enkerli (2019), *Son propre son: bricolage sonore et appropriation technologique*, *Anthropologie et Sociétés* 43(1)
**Level:** `read` — the full article.

**What it is.** Technological appropriation described through digital bricolage,
from a personal exploration of the Raspberry Pi's musical possibilities, situated
in Maker culture and nourished by prior ethnographic work (hunters' music in
Mali; knowledge transfer among Franco-Ontarian artisanes).

**What it argues.** Sound is largely absent from anthropology's field practice
despite the discipline's reliance on speech; the transposition of sound into text
is as untransparent now as the translation of culture into text was in 1986.
Maker culture's dominant ideology is a **meritocracy** — competitive, individualist,
with a note of essentialism — whose exclusions operate through what counts as
having done your homework. Bricolage is preferred to hybridity or créolisation
precisely because it **presupposes no prior classification**. And appropriation,
in the technopedagogical sense, is a way of making a knowledge one's own.

**Why it keeps returning.** Almost every position in this repo is already in this
article. Appropriation applies to frameworks as readily as to technology, which is
the answer to the competency-framework dissonance. The meritocracy critique is the
source of the no-failure-state instinct. Active listening at the Labo symphonique
is the answering-instrument form. And the diversity of sonic material — the "808
problem", tens of thousands of TR-808 samples against a few hundred clarinets —
is the diversity test.

**For MTILT.** This is the foundation document, and the project is its
continuation rather than a departure from it.

# N. Added from the local library, 2026-09-13

Three sources found in `references/pdf/` that were not in the bibliography. All
three are now in `mtilt.bib`. They share a theme, which is why they are grouped:
**each one is a case of the unmarked default being marked.**

### `gomez2013computational` — Gómez, Herrera & Gómez-Martin (2013), *Computational Ethnomusicology: perspectives and challenges*, *Journal of New Music Research* 42(2), 111–112
**Level:** `read` — it is a two-page editorial introducing a special issue.

**What it says, in its first sentence.** "Since the beginning of Music Information
Retrieval (MIR) as a field, most of its models and technologies have been
developed for mainstream popular music in the so-called 'Western' tradition." The
editorial proposes Computational Ethnomusicology as "a new mental framework that
helps to restructure problems" rather than as a new application area.

**For MTILT — this is the answer to a question asked earlier in the session.** The
MIR rundown question ("maybe there's a MIR rundown which encompasses many of
these") had two parts, and this is the second: not *what does MIR know about
representation*, but *whose music was MIR built on*. The field names its own
default here, from inside.

It is also the entry point to the "music labs around the world" note. A special
issue is a list of who is doing this work and where, and two pages of editorial is
a cheap way to get that list.

### `weisser2013qenet` — Weisser & Falceto (2013), *Investigating qəñət in Amhara secular music: an acoustic and historical study*, *Annales d'Ethiopie* 28, 299–322
**Level:** `landing` — abstract and front matter. Open access via Persée.

**What it is.** An acoustic and historical study of *qəñət*, the modal system of
Amhara secular music. Its abstract opens by naming what it is arguing against:
"Since the 1960's, a kind of musicological gospel claims that Amhara secular
music…" — a received account that the paper tests against measurement.

**Why it belongs here.** It is a worked instance of everything pitch-ontology is
for: a non-Western modal system, a claim that circulated as settled for fifty
years, and an acoustic study that treats the claim as checkable. Not "here is
another tuning system" but "here is how a musicological consensus about one gets
audited."

**For MTILT.** The most concrete `Situating` material in the whole library, and
in French and English both. Worth reading properly before the pitch-ontology work
goes further — it is a model for what a claim record with real evidence looks
like.

### `kubik2010theory` — Gerhard Kubik (2010), *Theory of African Music*, Volume II, University of Chicago Press
**Level:** `landing` — front matter and series information. 368 pages, unread.

**What it is.** The second volume of Kubik's theory of African music, in the
Chicago Studies in Ethnomusicology series (Bohlman, Nettl and Radano). Volume I
appeared in 1994 from F. Noetzel. Kubik is a cultural anthropologist,
ethnomusicologist and psychoanalyst, at Vienna and Klagenfurt, affiliated with the
Oral Literature Research Programme in Chileka, Malaŵi.

**Why it matters for this project specifically.** The rhythm material in `docs/01`
and `docs/02` leans on TUBS — a transcription grid devised *for* West African
drumming by outsiders — and on Toussaint's geometry, which is a mathematician's
account. Kubik is the theory from inside the scholarship of the music itself, at
length. If the rhythm-ring and TUBS material is going to be used with participants,
this is the source that can say where those tools distort.

**Unread, and the priority is high for that reason.** Two volumes of theory is the
kind of source that changes what you think a workshop on "the same eight steps"
is doing.
