## Threads worth pulling — Claude's annotations, not Alex's notes

Pointers and connections only. None of this is a decision.

### Toys instead of games — the sharpest note in the set

Caillois already drew this line. *Les jeux et les hommes* (1958) sets **paidia** —
free, tumultuous, improvised play — against **ludus**, play bound by conventions and
arbitrary difficulty, as two ends of one continuum. Gamification imports ludus
mechanics (points, levels, streaks, leaderboards) and loses paidia entirely, which is
exactly the "game mechanics without the fun of play" complaint. Will Wright called
SimCity a *software toy* rather than a game for the same reason: no win condition.

That gives a design question rather than a test: **does the thing have a win
condition?** If it does, it has smuggled the scoreboard back in — the same scoreboard
that "ungrading", "no guilty pleasures" and the objection to "sophistication" all
refuse. Toy-shaped is the consistent answer.

### Oregon Trail and the edutainment limit

Worth pairing with the note directly below it, since Oregon Trail is a journey game
whose whole pedagogy is hardship: scarcity, bad luck, dysentery. It works *because*
the failure is real. But the failure belongs to a fictional family, not to the player's
competence. That may be the line for "limits to edutainment in terms of what could be
painful" — **painful inside the fiction, never painful about the learner**. A game may
kill your oxen; it should not inform you that you are unmusical.

### (Self-)compassion

Neff's construct has three parts — self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness — and
the active ingredient for our purposes is *common humanity*: recognising a difficulty
as shared rather than as personal deficiency. Which is the same move as
"acknowledging what'll be difficult." Saying a climb is steep before anyone starts it
is a compassion practice, not just honesty. Directly relevant to the desire/skill ditch.

### ASK, SKA, KSAT

The standard HR ordering is KSA — knowledge, then skills, then attitudes. Inverting it
to **Attitudes → Skills → Knowledge** is a real pedagogical position, not a mnemonic
joke: attitudes gate whether anything else gets through. KSAT adds Tasks (or Tools),
and that is exactly where the ambivalence belongs — framing tools as "solutions"
presupposes a problem, and MTILT is mostly not solving problems. SKA stays in the
repo for morale.

### Hackspace, third space

Oldenburg's *The Great Good Place* (1989): the third place is neither home nor work,
has a low barrier to entry, treats conversation as the main activity, has regulars,
and runs on a playful mood. "Being a good hang" is close to a definition of third-place
behaviour. This connects straight back to the makerspace material in the 2019 article —
and it may be the venue answer for MTILT sessions, which have so far had no venue.

### Topographies, and trails between landscapes

Extends the landscape sketch usefully: not one terrain but several, with passes between
them. The trails would be the crossings — music↔language, music↔drawing, music↔dance —
which turns "connections between music and other domains" from a curriculum item into
a navigational feature. You travel between landscapes; you do not study the connection.

### Music and dance, embodiment

Godøy & Leman (eds.), *Musical Gestures: Sound, Movement, and Meaning* (2010) is the
collection; the phenomenology runs through Merleau-Ponty. Connects to DrawnQurve
(gesture becoming sound) and to the breath material. Also the most underexploited
crossing on the list, given how much of the suite is already gestural.

### Encyclopedia with practice before concept

Possibly the concrete shape of the whole build. An encyclopedia whose every entry
opens with something to do and only then names what happened — which is the
"experience then explain" scaffold turned into a format rather than a principle.
Each entry is a small module (H5P, or plain JS in the Building Blocks lineage), and
the collection grows one entry at a time in the manner of patch-panel and Manifold.
"Voiceleading through practice before explanation" and "singing without piano" are
both entries in it, not separate projects.

### The YouTube thread

There is a genre of music-theory video that reveals a genuine phenomenon and then
oversells it. The reveal is the good part and it is already a mass entry point.
Keeping the reveal and dropping the hype is a workable stance, and those videos
are a ready-made corpus of what people are already curious about.

### Drill and discipline

Sits in honest tension with everything around it, and the tension is worth keeping
rather than resolving. Some things need reps. The landscape sketch puts this under
peaks: mark the elevation, let people choose the climb, don't pretend the slope is
gentle.

### Zotero

Practical: `references/mtilt.bib` and `references/mtilt.csl.json` both import directly.

## Threads — Claude's annotations, not Alex's notes

### The through-line of this batch is plurality, not neutrality

Nearly every note here refuses a single correct version: several hammers, several modes
of relating to music, several landscapes with trails between them, several labs beyond
North America and Europe, transmodality across senses. **Multiplying experiences** is
the stated version. This is a sharper position than "escape the defaults," because it
does not pretend an escape is available.

### The hex grid concession corrects the decolonizing mode

The landscape sketch flagged "substituting one default for another" as the risk of
decolonizing. The note is sharper than that flag: a hex grid *is* as much a Golden
Hammer as a piano, and the value was never neutrality. It is **difference** — having
more than one hammer, and knowing each one's grip. Which means the decolonizing mode
is not about finding the unmarked instrument. It is about never having only one.
`docs/05-landscape.md` should be amended on this point.

### TaMilDaA, taken literally

"Talking about music is like dancing about architecture" is catalogued by Alan P. Scott,
whose page is literally at `tamildaa.htm` — the source of the acronym, and the same
Scott 2010 cited in the 2019 article. Its attribution is famously unresolved (Martin
Mull, Elvis Costello, Steve Martin, Laurie Anderson, Frank Zappa, Thelonious Monk),
which is part of why it works as a put-down: nobody has to own it.

Taking it as an invitation rather than a dismissal is the transmodal move. And the
dismissal has an obvious flaw once you look at it: **dancing about architecture is a
perfectly good thing to do.** It is site-specific dance, and it exists. The aphorism
only stings if you assume the translation must be lossless — which is exactly the
assumption `docs/02` spends its length dismantling for notation.

### Xenakis closes that loop by himself

He trained and worked as an architect with Le Corbusier, and the Philips Pavilion
(Brussels, 1958) and *Metastaseis* share their generating idea: string glissandi and
ruled surfaces are the same geometry, realised once in sound and once in concrete.
He is already in the references for UPIC. So the architecture↔music crossing has a
figure who did it in both directions, seriously, and left the drawings.

Also the cleanest available answer to "when is a crossing real" — Xenakis was not
illustrating music with architecture. It was one object, built twice.

### The local model question is a curriculum-definition device

"What would I teach a naïve musicker?" is doing double duty. Choosing a training corpus
*is* choosing a curriculum, and the sequence sketched — call & response, sampling,
compressors, semi-generative melodies and beats — is already an ordering that starts
from social and gestural material and reaches production tools late. That is the descent
and ascent from `docs/03` in different clothing. Worth writing down as a syllabus even
if no model ever gets trained.

### Four modes of relating to music — a replacement for the learner profile

Spectator sport, commodity, body practice, space to explore, all valid for different
people **at different times**. That last clause does the work. It is a description of
what somebody is doing *right now* rather than of who they are, which makes it the
non-ranking alternative to a learner profile — closer to the trail log than to a scale.
A person can move between all four in one afternoon.

### Proto-personas already in the repos

Worth gathering. Personas written for a tool are usually more honest than personas
written for a pedagogy, because they had to justify a design decision.

### Music labs beyond the usual map

Named as a gap, and it is a decolonizing task in the literal sense — the map of who
studies music with technology is itself surveyed from two continents. Candidates worth
scouting: institutions in Brazil, India, Japan, Senegal, South Africa, Indonesia,
Mexico. This would be an actual research task rather than a note.

### Huizinga alongside Caillois

*Homo Ludens* (1938) is already cited in the 2019 article — Caillois wrote partly in
response to it, so having both is having the argument rather than one side of it.
Huizinga's play is culture-generating and serious; Caillois's typology and the
paidia/ludus continuum come from disagreeing with him about how much order play needs.

### Curation in music, as a first-person corpus

Pieces to practice, styles enjoyed, beat patterns, samples, concepts. This is the
information-architecture instinct pointed at oneself, and it is the same object as the
Zotero/Raindrop idea — a curated set with files, archives and annotations. One schema
could hold both: the reference manager and the sample library are the same problem.

### Music and time perception

Genuinely underexploited. Music is the most available instrument anyone has for
noticing that subjective duration is not clock duration — a groove that makes four
minutes vanish, a held note that will not end. Sits with the phasing material in
`docs/01`.

## Threads — Claude's annotations, not Alex's notes

### These are one lineage, not four notes

HLO S.A. — Herrmutt Lobby — made **Beatsurfing** (iPad, 2012, with Yaniv De
Ridder) and **Playground** (2016). Same interaction primitive in both: you do not
press things, you find paths *across* them. Beatsurfing has you draw your own
controller and surf it; Playground has you glide and swipe to "find paths across
buttons that sound interesting", with shakers you grab and move so they strike
other objects. That is *curves and gestures* as a design language rather than as
a feature.

And Beatsurfing shipped a **two-player collaborative update**. So the tools found
inspiring and the nut that never cracked were the same team's problem, in the
same design language, more than a decade ago.

### Why the nut resists, in one line

Endlesss closed in May 2024 — Tim Exile's platform, the most serious attempt at
multiplayer loop jamming, with real funding and a real community. The pattern is
worth naming honestly: **networked music tools keep solving the wrong half.**
They solve *sharing loops* and call it playing together. What is easy in person
is not the audio path; it is the turn-taking, the glance, the willingness to drop
out, the fact that nobody is recording. Latency gets blamed because it is
measurable.

The MTILT-shaped version of this might not be a networked tool at all. If being
a good hang is a value and third places are the venue, the honest answer to
"sync and play together" may be **face-to-face, with the online case treated as
the hard degraded mode rather than the target.**

### Curating tools as a repo

"What I find fun, useful, effective, inspiring" is the same shape as
`patch-panel`, and a sibling to it: patch-panel is a cabinet of modular systems,
this would be a cabinet of *tools that teach something by how they feel to use*.
Same conventions — evidence-tagged, CC0 data, status ladder. HLO Playground,
Beatsurfing, Learning Synths, Syntorial and Building Blocks are the first five
entries and they are already characterised across `docs/02` through `docs/05`.

Note that "fun, useful, effective, inspiring" are four different axes and a tool
can score on one without the others. Worth keeping them separate rather than
averaging — the same argument as curation in passes.

### Collating the repos

Done, as `docs/06-repo-insights.md`. The short version: most of MTILT's stated
positions are already implemented in the repos, years before they were
articulated as a pedagogy. Curation-in-passes is the anti-ranking machinery,
already built. The absences are consistent across all 52 — and **no multi-user
anything anywhere**, which confirms the note above from a different direction.

## Threads — Claude's annotations, not Alex's notes

Followed in full in `docs/09-participatory-discrepancies.md`. Short version:

**These are one thread, and Keil tied it in 1987.** *Participatory Discrepancies
and the Power of Music*, Cultural Anthropology 2(3): "the power of music is in its
participatory discrepancies, and these are basically of two kinds: processual and
textural." Processual is timing — swing, push, the drummer's delay. **Textural is
pitch and timbre, and Keil's own example is paired instruments producing blended
harmonics.** That is the "blending" from the conversation, named, and put in the
same frame as the timing.

Keil's mechanism is the part that matters for MTILT: discrepancies work *outside
conscious rational analysis*, their meanings stay "various and ambiguous", and the
ambiguity is what gives them "active participatory power". And his line on
notation — that it preserves only a **"petrified skeleton"** — lands beside
Seeger's first hazard.

**The collaboration nut, re-read.** If the power is in the discrepancies, then
networked music tools have been solving the wrong half: they quantise, snap, align
and sync, which removes the thing. A better hypothesis than latency, and testable.

**And the complication is the best part.** Keil's claim is contested by controlled
studies — Frühauf/Kopiez/Platz, Davies/Madison/Silva/Gouyon, Senn et al. 2016,
Datseris et al. 2019 — which repeatedly fail to find that microtiming deviations
raise groove ratings, and sometimes find quantised versions rated higher. Câmara
et al. 2023 and a *Revisiting Charles Keil* commentary in Empirical Musicology
Review keep the argument live.

**Two claims must not be run together.** Keil's is about *expressive deviation
from a norm*. Polak & London's is that Mande subdivision is *categorically
non-isochronous* — 41:31:28 — with Long/Short as discrete metrical categories.
The lab studies test the first and do not touch the second. Quantising an uneven
meter does not remove feel; it changes the meter.

**Lascabettes** is a postdoc at Strasbourg (PhD IRCAM/Sorbonne, editorial board of
the Journal of Mathematics and Music) working on the geometry of musical rhythms,
with the Mathémusique outreach channel and an interactive site, **rhythm-circle.com**.
Belongs in the tool curation — and is the clearest possible illustration of
`docs/02`'s argument, since a ring places onsets at exact angles and therefore
cannot show a participatory discrepancy at all.

**Improvisation for classical musicians** turns out to have a sharp literature:
Dolan et al., "The Improvisational State of Mind" (Frontiers 2018); "Are Classical
Musicians Excluded from Improvisation? Cultural Hegemony and the Effects of
Ideology" (Contemporary Music Review 2021); and "Challenging score-centered norms
in Western classical higher music education." That last is this project's position
appearing inside conservatoire research — useful for the translation work.

**Angine de Poitrine** — Saguenay band, from Saint-Fulgence, two volumes, covered
by Radio-Canada, Le Devoir and Urbania, on Bandcamp. Listening material with a
local connection, which matters for the "known sounds" principle.
